# Evidence-bearing video review

Review each selected example and a relevant comparison where possible. Save first-frame literal observation separately from the inferred scroll-stopper. Record the promise at 0-5 seconds and when the promised result arrives.

Required axes: context/creator/product/date; first frame; hook/promise; format and scene order; message; product integration/time; pacing/cuts/transitions/repeats; subtitles/overlays; voice; music; CTA inside video, in speech and in caption; comments; alternative explanations; practical adaptation.

For each axis store status (observed, transcribed_only, sampled, unavailable, not_reviewed, not_applicable), evidence URL/local frame and time span, observation and inference separately. Exact hook quotes require checked text/voice, otherwise paraphrase.

Use continuous supported video review where possible. For frame inspection inspect the opening and transitions densely, then scenes across the full duration. State sampling step. Do not claim text/CTA absent throughout a video when only intermittent frames were examined. Caption instructions are not on-screen instructions.

Check actual audio stream and synchronization, then use a supported audio-analysis/listening capability if available. ASR only provides recognition; empty/low-confidence transcript does not establish no voice. Separate speech, sung lyrics, music and sound effects only where supported. Do not infer voice timbre/emotion, beat alignment or sonic hooks from a title/waveform/ASR alone. Mark these unresolved and supply the local source for a reviewer when needed.

Annotate subtitle type (semantic headings vs verbatim speech), readability, position, timing; actor framing, setting, camera movement and garment behavior when relevant. Do not attribute a transformation to fit if hair, pose, lighting and accessories changed too.

Comments are corroborating observations, not causal truth. Identify what receives attention: item, creator, competition, topic or confusion. Price questions may show missing information as well as interest. Explain how each finding changes a shootable hypothesis for the user's actual product.
