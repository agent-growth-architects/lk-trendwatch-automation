# Research method

## Brief and population

Use [onboarding.md](onboarding.md) for a new brand. Record business, product/service, audience/geo/language, platform, growth or commercial goal, traffic path, inventory or service capacity, creator/resources, prior own-account results and approved profiles. Do not force unrelated app/AI-production questions. Unknown cost/schedule is unknown, not zero or an invented commitment.

Registry fields: name, handle, platform, profile_url, verification_source, role, inclusion_reason, status, approved (explicit boolean). Additional brand_id or access_status metadata may be retained in project evidence. Select a manageable shortlist from the brief; no fixed account count or inherited brand list is required. Proposed entries start with approved: false; only explicit approval changes that status. Preserve unresolved approved brands. Partners are separate profiles, not extra approved brands. Detect possible partnership/ownership as a hypothesis unless supported by explicit evidence. One placement is not grounds to discard an author. Account size affects context/confidence, not an automatic universal ban.

For the selected window, collect chronological posts through its boundary or the declared cap. Keep completeness per profile and unobserved/unknown dates. Report: approved profiles, identity-confirmed, accessible, numeric rows, dated rows, fresh rows, page reviews, full audiovisual reviews. Do not conflate these levels.

## Comparison

Use a declared baseline sample, ideally observed at comparable age, platform/surface and distribution context. Exclude the target itself when comparing against other posts. If excluding pinned/out-of-order items, record which and why. Include N, IDs, age span and coverage limitations. Do not call mixed-age lifetime medians age-matched lift.

For a collaboration, distinguish the publication owner, brand grid and partner grids. A brand repost need not share an author's baseline. Followers are a contextual snapshot; views/followers is not ER or unique reach. Do not infer conversion from saves or retention from reactions. Average watch time/duration is not completion rate and can include repeat viewing.

Select transferable outliers plus ordinary/weak comparison examples with similar subject/format/author/age where available. A matched observational pair helps generate explanations; it does not isolate causality. Keep alternative explanations: topic demand, product scarcity, launch, creator equity, giveaway, paid partnership, cross-platform distribution, age, production difference. For one-variable tests, hold the remaining feasible elements constant; changes of audience still limit causal conclusions.

## Three separate judgments

Signal: observable deviation, repeated occurrence, growth over time.
Confidence: source completeness, sampling size, date precision, media review, confounding.
Transferability: audience/task/product fit, available inventory, actual resources, repeatability without borrowed celebrity demand.

Explain each in words; do not collapse into a fabricated weighted impact score. Use own historical outcomes to support or contradict a recommendation. A single failure does not permanently blacklist a format.

## Freshness versus growth

Fresh: confirmed date within window.
Repeated pattern: recognizable mechanism across independent originals; count unique authors/posts, do not count coauthors or copies as independent replication.
Growth signal: timestamped comparable observations. Audio-use growth is different from view growth of an example. Format adoption needs new occurrences and the sampled population denominator across periods. Neither one fresh post nor one popular audio count establishes lifecycle.

Respect rounding resolution and tiny baselines. Show raw counts and time intervals before percentage changes. Negative/revised counts are data-quality flags, not automatically declining trends. No observations means unknown. Brand-specific heuristics are opt-in and user-approved; the default is none. Keep their approval context and label decision use separately from observed evidence.
